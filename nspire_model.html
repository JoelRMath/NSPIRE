NSPIRE Inspection Optimization Model

Author

Joël R. Pradines, Ph.D.

Published

June 9, 2026

1. Introduction

This document outlines the foundational mathematical model for optimizing maintenance resource allocation ahead of an NSPIRE (National Standards for the Physical Inspection of Real Estate) inspection. The objective is to maximize the expected effective score by traversing a dependency-constrained network of tasks, evaluated via Monte Carlo simulations to account for operational stochastics.

2. The NSPIRE Scoring Mechanism

The inspection divides a property into distinct locations and evaluates defects based on severity.

2.1. Definitions and Sets

  • Severity: \(s \in \{\text{LT}, \text{S}, \text{STD}\}\) (Life-Threatening, Severe, Standard)
  • Location: \(l \in \{\text{Unit}, \text{Inside}, \text{Outside}\}\)
  • \(N\): Total number of units in the property.
  • \(n\): Number of units inspected.
  • \(C_{s,l}\): True total count of defects existing in the property.
  • \(c_{s,l}\): Observed count of defects recorded during the inspection.
  • \(w_{s,l}\): Static point weight (penalty) assigned to a defect.

2.2. The Deduction Function

The total point deduction \(D\) is a summation over all severities and locations, normalized by the sample size:

\[D = \sum_{s \in \{\text{LT}, \text{S}, \text{STD}\}} \sum_{l \in \{\text{Unit}, \text{Inside}, \text{Outside}\}} \left( \frac{c_{s,l}}{n} \cdot w_{s,l} \right)\]

2.3. The Raw Continuous Score

The base score originates at 100 and is bounded at zero using the Heaviside step function \(H(x)\):

\[S_{raw} = (100 - D) \cdot H(100 - D)\]

2.4. The Operational Compliance Function (Failsafe)

A score \(\ge 60\) mathematically passes, but the presence of any Life-Threatening (LT) defect forces an immediate 24-hour mitigation compliance failure. Let \(n_{LT}\) represent the total observed LT defects:

\[n_{LT} = \sum_{l} c_{\text{LT},l}\]

\[S_{eff} = S_{raw} \cdot H(0.5 - n_{LT})\]

3. Probabilistic Sampling and Expected Deductions

Unit defects are sampled without replacement from \(N\). Thus, \(c_{s,\text{Unit}}\) follows a hypergeometric distribution.

The Expected Value of observed unit defects is: \[E[c_{s,\text{Unit}}] = n \cdot \frac{C_{s,\text{Unit}}}{N}\]

Substituting this into the Expected Total Deduction \(E[D]\):

\[E[D] = \sum_{s} \left[ \frac{C_{s,\text{Inside}} \cdot w_{s,\text{Inside}}}{n} + \frac{C_{s,\text{Outside}} \cdot w_{s,\text{Outside}}}{n} + \frac{C_{s,\text{Unit}} \cdot w_{s,\text{Unit}}}{N} \right]\]

4. Resource Mapping and Graph Topology (DAG)

Maintenance tasks form a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) where each task \(i\) is a vertex \(V_i\) with an expected execution time \(t_i\) and material cost \(m_i\).

4.1. Edge Constraints

  1. Sequential Precedence: A directed edge \(V_A \to V_B\) requires the start time of \(B\) to be \(\ge\) the end time of \(A\).
  2. Temporal Blocking: Certain vertices impose a local resource freeze, locking a spatial node for a time interval \(\Delta t\).
  3. Mutually Exclusive Pruning: If a higher-order vertex \(V_{replace}\) is activated, redundant child vertices \(V_{repair}\) are pruned (\(t_{repair} \to 0\)).

5. Task Modalities & Execution Constraints

Task execution times are not normally distributed; they represent distinct physical realities (e.g., a quick adjustment versus a catastrophic replacement).

5.1. Mixture Distributions (PERT)

Task \(i\) exists in state \(k \in \{1, 2, 3\}\). The execution time \(t_i\) is drawn from a bounded Beta-PERT distribution, weighted by the physical probability of the state (\(p_{i,k}\)):

\[f(t_i) = \sum_{k} p_{i,k} \cdot \text{PERT}(t; a_k, m_k, b_k)\] where \(a_k, m_k, b_k\) represent the optimistic, mode, and pessimistic completion times.

5.2. Vendor Handoff Nodes

If a task evaluates to a severe state requiring external licensing or specialization (e.g., licensed plumbing or glazing), the node logic bifurcates: * Internal labor time drops to zero (\(t_{internal} \to 0\)). * A schedule lag is injected, drawing from a Uniform distribution: \(L \sim \text{Uniform}(l_{min}, l_{max})\) days. * A mandatory spatial lockout is triggered (e.g., 24-hour cure time for silicone), forcing the DAG to re-route internal technicians.

6. Quality Control & Redundancy

Execution of a task does not guarantee absolute resolution. A task has a baseline probability (\(p_{err}\)) of being missed or improperly repaired.

  • Serial Execution (Single Technician): The probability of a defect surviving into the inspection phase is \(P(\text{Survive}) = p_{err}\).
  • Parallel Execution (Dual Technicians): Applying the “Four Eyes” principle, independent cross-checking drastically reduces error. The joint probability of both technicians missing the defect becomes \(P(\text{Survive}) = p_{err}^2\).

Surviving defects directly populate the observed defect count \(c_{s,l}\) in the NSPIRE scoring function.

7. Predictive Topological Risk (Latent Covariates)

Baseline task probabilities assume a standard asset deterioration rate. However, lack of historical tenant reporting indicates hidden deferred maintenance, warping the DAG’s risk topology.

Let \(W_u\) be the count of historical service requests for unit \(u\). The Unit Risk Multiplier \(R_u \in (0, 1]\) follows an exponential decay function:

\[R_u = e^{-\lambda W_u}\]

Where \(\lambda\) is a tuning parameter. For units with low reporting (\(W_u \to 0\), \(R_u \to 1\)), probability mass is dynamically shifted from the nominal state (\(p_1\)) to the catastrophic/vendor state (\(p_3\)) bounded by a maximum shift factor \(\Delta_{max}\):

\[p_3^* = p_3 + (R_u \cdot \Delta_{max})\] \[p_1^* = \max(0, p_1 - (R_u \cdot \Delta_{max}))\]

This transforms the engine from a purely stochastic simulator into a predictive prioritization tool for pre-inspection targeting.
